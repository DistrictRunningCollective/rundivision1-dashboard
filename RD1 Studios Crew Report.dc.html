Belonging is the metric that moves the needle.

PEACE RUNNERS 773's Out West Run 13.1 training program in Chicago's Garfield Park just produced one of the first studies to measure a community running program's health, social, and economic effects together — and belonging came out as the driver behind all of it.

The study

PEACE RUNNERS 773 partnered with SPARC for Sports, which brought in researchers Dr. Jennifer Wiggins-Lyndall and Dr. Shawn Rohlin of Kent State University to study the crew's Out West Run 13.1 training program. The resulting Comprehensive Impact Report set out to connect health data, economic data, and the stories of runners and their neighborhoods in one place — something Wiggins-Lyndall said had not been attempted for a community running program before.

The findings held up across four areas: mental health, physical activity, workplace productivity, and community connection. Participants improved by an average of 2.6 points on the PHQ-9 depression scale, moving from mild to minimal depressive symptoms, and that shift alone was tied to an estimated $3,200 to $3,279 in workplace productivity gains per person each year.

Why belonging, not miles

The most consistent theme in the data wasn't the running itself. In interviews, participants described feeling supported, feeling like they weren't alone, and feeling part of something bigger — and researchers pointed to that sense of belonging as the thread running through nearly every gain the study measured.

Researchers also flagged a network effect: runners who received support through the program went on to become a source of encouragement for others, spreading the program's benefits through Garfield Park over time rather than stopping with the people who showed up first.

"What surprised us in the qualitative data is that participants kept crediting the community more than the running itself."

— Dr. Jennifer Wiggins-Lyndall, Kent State University

What it means for the network

For RD1, this is a working example of what the Impact Cup is built to recognize: documented, measurable community impact around a circuit, not just turnout on race day. PEACE RUNNERS 773's report gives other crews a framework for evaluating their own health, social, and economic impact — and gives RD1 judges harder evidence to score against.
